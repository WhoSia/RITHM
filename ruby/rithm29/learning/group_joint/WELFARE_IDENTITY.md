# Six-driver congestion: exact welfare identity
Let n denote number taking A (0..6); other 6-n take B. Source experiment's cost A=n, B=2(6-n). Thus average cost C(n)=[n*n+2(6-n)^2]/6=4+(n-4)^2/2. This is an identity, not a fitted regression. Total group cost=24+3(n-4)^2. All 5,760 source group-period records independently satisfy equality to floating-point exactness. Therefore group welfare loss relative to optimal n=4 is one half the **second moment** E[(N-4)^2], which decomposes into one half [Var(N)+(E N-4)^2]. Predicting the mean group count alone cannot certify cost forecasts; a distribution with a correct mean and too much variance overstates expected congestion cost.
