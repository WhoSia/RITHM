## 2. Exactly observed versus not identified from occupancy

Let `a` and `b` be the number on route A in two consecutive *periods*. Define `U` the number of B→A transitions, `D` A→B, and total person switches `G=U+D`. Then

`b-a = U-D`, `G=|b-a|+2 min(U,D)`.

The **sharp occupancy fiber** of physically possible total switches is

`G ∈ {|b-a|, |b-a|+2, ..., min(a+b, 12-a-b)}`.

Proof: between one period and the next, `D` can range from `max(0,a-b)` to `min(a,6-b)`; `U=b-a+D`; each resulting integer choice is realizable by selecting exactly `D` of the `a` prior A occupants and exactly `U` of the `6-a` prior B occupants to switch. The resulting endpoints and parity are necessary and sufficient. Every one of the 7×7 feasible pairs and all labelled six-bit masks were independently enumerated in tests to confirm this identity; no learning or update-order hypothesis is required.

Two explicit macro-indistinguishable labelled kernels, given each occupancy transition `(a,b)`:

* **Minimum-churn** selects only labelled transitions achieving `G=|b-a|`.
* **Uniform identity reassignment** selects a next-period `b`-subset uniformly among all `C(6,b)` subsets, independently of the previous identity configuration conditional on `b`. Its exact switching distribution is `P(G=g | a,b) = C(a,D) C(6-a,U)/C(6,b)`, with `D=(g-b+a)/2` and `U=(g+b-a)/2`, and `E[G | a,b]=(a(6-b)+(6-a)b)/6`.

The original labelled path is a *third*, **observed** point in the occupancy fiber, not a third model assumed generated by either benchmark kernel. These three constructions agree on the chosen occupancy path and therefore on `24+3(b-4)^2` stage congestion cost. They disagree on `G`. Consequently, even an oracle that knows the entire occupancy path or the exact macro transition law need not know the actor-specific switch law or the switch-resource-cost term. The same result holds over full occupancy paths by assembling a permissible labelled assignment at each period sequentially.

**Hard counterexample:** fix occupancy at A=4 throughout 40 periods. One complete labelled path never changes anybody (39-transition total `G=0`); another alternates the two masks `001111` and `111100`, causing 4 legitimate person switches per period interval (`G=156`), despite identical period-by-period occupancy and zero excess congestion throughout. If the incremental resource cost per switch is `κ>0`, their realized 40-period cost differs by exactly `156κ`. This is not a claim that any real human group followed the contrived alternating path.

### 4:2 remains efficient in occupancy but not automatically in churn

In 1,067 transitions with A occupancy 4 both before and after, actual `G=0/2/4` occurred **682/350/35** times. The uniform-label kernel would assign these classes exact conditional probabilities **1/15, 8/15, 2/5**, respectively, versus empirical proportions ~63.92% / 32.80% / 3.28%. It is an intentionally strong *label-shuffling null*, not a fitted behavioral model; its discrepancy establishes persistent *identity matching*, not that individual cognitive inertia is uniquely responsible. Stable personal tastes, experienced payoff learning, context, serially dependent common shocks and strategic interactions remain candidate mechanisms. The repeated measurements within each of 24 sessions are not iid Bernoulli samples.

For a **hypothetically supplied** incremental resource cost `κ≥0` per observed actor switch, set each group-section's additive score to `sum_{t=1}^{40} 3(n_{A,t}-4)^2 + κ sum_{t=2}^{40} G_t`. The original experiment's stage baseline `24*40` cancels in comparisons. Observed total-score difference *per group section* is, with exact rational coefficients:

* **Partial − No**: `−61/16 + (41/48)κ`. This finite sample's sign flips at `κ=183/41≈4.4634` score-units per actor switch.
* **Full − No**: `−19/4 − (77/8)κ`. The point-ranked observed sample difference is negative for all `κ≥0`.
* **Full − Partial**: `−15/16 − (503/48)κ`, also negative at the point-ranked sample level for `κ≥0`.

**No external price for switching has been estimated.** These equations are arithmetic on original participant behavior under historically different information treatment conditions. They do **not** establish the causal welfare effect of implementing an intervention; subject-specific potential outcomes, randomization unit, ordering, between-section interference, and session-level sampling uncertainty require their own research design.

### Session-level sensitivity and selection

There are 24 shared sessions and all three treatment arms represented within each. Importantly, each session has **1, 2, or 3 group sections per arm**, though the three arm counts match *within* a session. R55 therefore draws entire sessions with replacement and computes a *ratio of total group-section score sums to total group-section counts*, rather than giving sessions of different sizes equal normalized weight. Results below use 8,000 seeded whole-session draws, 2.5%/97.5% descriptive resampling percentiles, **not causal or population confidence intervals**:

| Observed finite-cohort difference per group section | Point difference | Whole-session resampling percentile band |
|---|---:|---|
| Partial − No, congestion | −3.8125 | [−17.1333, +9.8000] |
| Partial − No, gross switches | +0.8542 | [−4.5435, +6.1087] |
| Full − No, congestion | −4.7500 | [−18.5510, +9.4200] |
| Full − No, gross switches | −9.6250 | [−15.3600, −3.2222] |

Even where the observed group-section aggregate score ranks Full below No for every nonnegative κ, the resampling distribution shows that the congestion difference alone is not stable enough to assert a general or causal information policy advantage. Gross switching is distinctly different as a measured axis. The sample is purposefully not generalized to unobserved populations without a sampling model.
