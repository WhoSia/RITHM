# RITHM-2.10 P3-R59 — Prior-Exposure, Latent Heterogeneity & Free-Running Welfare Transport

**2026-11-21 KST · Original-author-approved 24-session retrospective analysis · no new human recruitment.**

## Research-director verdict

**Do not promote the R58 one-step logloss ranking into an autonomous 40-round congestion or information-policy result.** Under actual initial routes, SSM1–9 has **higher** 40-round group-level congestion prediction MSE than the private-history model; under author-style fair initial routes, its congestion prediction MSE is **lower**. The apparent fair-start pooled congestion calibration **conceals the opposite Full-versus-No treatment ranking**. Its movement prediction error is higher than private-history's under both starts. An external per-switch price changes the joint resource-MSE ranking, without estimating that price or a causal information-policy effect.

A separate original-human retrospective link provides unusually strong **predictive evidence of persistent individual route-change heterogeneity across sequential experimental sections**, even against permutation of actors *within the same six-person group*. The true origin of this persistence remains unresolved: permanent individual tendencies, experience carried across sections, common institutional environment and learning could coexist. The author's Appendix E already reports carryover/order effects in the No condition's third segment, which must not be treated as new in this research.

### Two distinct free-running regimes

1. **Observed-initial closed loop:** fix each heldout group's observed period-1 actor routes, then generate all 39 later periods endogenously. This is a conditional rollout, not a full prospective origin model.
2. **Fair-initial closed loop:** all six participants draw their first route from 50:50, then **all 39 future decisions are generated by their own previous route/payoff experience**. The original Appendix B prescribes a uniform period-1 route. It is intentionally different from the observed t=1 path.

At every round, the six participants choose **simultaneously**. Congestion and their individual period-specific Values are revealed **after** the decision. Primary simulation replays each person's actual external `Value(t)` 6.0–16.0 tape *only as an exogenous after-action shock*, not as a forecast input. A separate fully simulated exogenous scenario draws new Values uniformly from the 101 one-decimal values `[6.0, ..., 16.0]`. These are two **different** environment assumptions. The primary tape is conditioned on authorized original realized exogenous shocks, not a claim that future unobserved values have been identified.

## 2. Model update and source-faithfulness contracts

- **EWA-lite**: native streaming state with attraction initial values `A_A(0)=A_B(0)=1`, observation-equivalent `N(0)=1`, self-tuning φ computed from an informed actor's realized previous other-A count or the **uninformed actor's own received-payoff-implied belief over counts**, attention δ, and a training-fold-only λ. Streaming attractions reproduce R58's **offline original Appendix A implementation at each of 40 steps** in adversarial source-free tests.
- **SSM5**: q=5 sampled with replacement from actual past paired payoffs (informed), or from separately experienced A and B payoff pools (uninformed). Until an uninformed actor has tried both routes, its decision remains a fair coin, exactly as Appendix B. Ties are random at probability 1/2. The optional lapse ε=.02 is a **researcher-added variant for continuity with the R58 one-step score**, NOT part of the original author SSM. Separate ε=0 reproduces the source's unmodified selection logic.
- **SSM1–9**: q_i is sampled uniformly once per participant and remains fixed throughout the **generated** 40-round trajectory, not freshly drawn each period; this respects the author's fixed-type simulation. R58's posterior mixture conditioned on actual human choices is **not** transplanted into the generated process. No unrealized foregone payoff is available to uninformed actors.
- **Private history**: actor probability uses only its own previous route, streak-length bin, received profit bin, informed status and fixed treatment, with learned counts from **other sessions**. Group occupancy is used afterwards to score costs, not as an input to these actors.

All predictions are source-free-testable with synthetic histories, including future-shock mutation tests: changing a `Value(t)` cannot change the simultaneous route decisions *at* or *before* period t. Informed-mask changes during a within-section rollout raise an error.

## 3. Exact theorem: one-step agreement does not determine autonomous resource use

Take two six-seat binary route masks M0 with 4 people on A and M1 with 5. All observed historical masks are M0. Both kernels agree on the entire observed support: from M0, choose M0 with probability 0.9, M1 with probability 0.1. **Reset** returns from M1 to M0 with probability 1; **Trap** stays at M1 with probability 1. Under the observed sequence all M0, every one-step conditional log-loss is **identical** in both worlds (`40×−log(.9)=4.2144206263`). Their unobserved off-path behavior differs.

An exact rational Markov calculation from M0 gives the **40-step expected post-period excess congestion** 10.9338843 under Reset versus **93.3990838 under Trap**; expected gross switches 7.1983471 versus **0.9852191**, respectively. Trap has *less* actor switching but *more* persistent congestion. All these values are from a constructed **source-free counterexample**, never historical measurements. It illustrates a standard exposure/distribution-shift issue already studied in sequential prediction (e.g. Ross–Gordon–Bagnell 2011 and Bengio et al. 2015); generic nonidentification/exposure bias is not a novelty claim for RITHM.

## 4. Original 24-session autonomous results

**Estimand:** per heldout group, predict the **40-period total** `sum_t 3(nA_t−4)^2` and `sum_{t=2}^{40} G_t`. Score the **square of the difference between the group-specific Monte-Carlo mean forecast and that group's observed total**; average those squared errors across 144 group-sections. This is *not* logloss, full trajectory likelihood, or actual policy welfare.

All primary scenarios: **32 Monte Carlo repetitions for each of 144 sections per model**, fixed seed 20261121, λ fit on 23 other sessions, and SSM optional lapse `.02`. Monte Carlo noise is explicitly reported separately from the 24-cluster descriptive resampling. A second independent seed and fresh-independent Value sensitivity are available in the aggregate JSON.

### B. Pooled match conceals opposite treatment response

The actual **40-period excess congestion per group** is No **123.4375**, Partial **119.6250**, Full **118.6875** (Full−No **−4.7500**). Fair-initial SSM1–9 predicts No about **105.82**, Partial **111.44**, Full **142.90** (Full−No **+37.08**), while the pooled predicted average excess is **120.05**, close to the observed **120.58**. This *pooled match is driven by opposite treatment-specific errors*. Its Full−No contrast error is **+41.83** with whole-session resampling range **[+26.69,+56.60]**. Observed-first SSM1–9 still predicts Full−No **+23.99**, an error of **+28.74** (descriptive range [+3.14,+52.97]).

The unmodified, zero-lapse fair-start SSM1–9 also predicts Full−No **+33.36**; uniformly freshly generated exogenous Values under ε=.02 produce **+36.61**. Thus the sign discrepancy persists under two specified sensitivity checks. It is not appropriate to call this proof that real information causally increases congestion: these are **simulation differences across distinct historically assigned treatment groups**, evaluated against original observational assignment outcomes. The historical Full−No point contrast itself is not sign-stable under earlier 24-session resampling of congestion.

### C. Switching price changes the predictive MSE ranking

The hypothetical resource score from *observable period-end* congestion and actual person-switch count is `W(κ)=Σ_t 3(nA_t−4)^2+κ Σ_{t=2}^{40}G_t`, where **κ≥0 is unestimated**. For model forecast errors `e_C`, `e_G` on group totals, the squared-error polynomial is exactly `A + B κ + D κ²`, where `B=2 mean(e_C e_G)`.

Under fair initial routes and the primary seed, subtracting the private-history forecast MSE from the SSM1–9 MSE gives:

`ΔMSE_SSM−Private(κ) = −643.1171 + 515.6362 κ + 241.4922 κ²`.

Its **nonnegative crossover is κ≈0.8825**. Thus for the *specific finite-sample prediction-loss functional* SSM1–9 is more accurate on κ=0 congestion alone but loses at sufficiently high penalties on gross switching. Independent seed and fresh-Value variants move this *prediction error threshold* to approximately **0.7217** and **1.0701**. These numbers are **not a recommended monetary price or a measured cognitive/switching resource parameter**. At κ=1 the SSM−private difference is +114.0, but the 24-session resampling range includes zero [−871.85,+998.43], preventing an overconfident general ranking.

## 5. Section-to-section human identity persistence; conditional negative control

The 24 retained sessions each have participants returning for subsequent sections. There are **48 adjacent section pairs** and **576 actor-section links** with exactly the same actor identities and unchanged six-person group assignments across adjacent sections; all transitions change treatment, and information status changes on 2/3 of individual links. The actual fraction choosing a different route at the section boundary is **28.82%**; the preceding section's last-period switch rate is **24.48%** and the next section's first internal transition **23.78%** (different time contexts, not causal effects).

Each person's 39-transition route-switch frequency from a section predicts their same person's 39-transition frequency in the following section with **Pearson r=0.61099** across 576 linked pairs. This is a *descriptive* correlation; 576 person-section links are **not** 576 independent clusters. Whole-session resampling (24 session units, 4,000 draws) gives **[0.51385,0.68829]**. More importantly, under a randomized label reassignment **within each identical six-person group at each adjacent section boundary**, preserving group/treatment/segment totals and actor-rate marginals, the 95% permutation reference interval is **[0.05541,0.20652]** (4,000 permutations). Zero of 4,000 within-group shuffled assignments attained or exceeded the observed correlation; the continuity-corrected permutation upper reference probability is **1/4,001≈0.000250** under that **specific exchangeable-label null**. This does *not* prove permanent cognitive/personality types or a causal carryover; persistent skill, stable preferences, latent attention, experience memory, practice and group/subject interactions are not separated by this test. The different treatment at every segment boundary particularly precludes naive before/after causal interpretation.

**Critical prior-art reconciliation:** the author's Appendix E already shows order/carryover evidence in the **No treatment at segment 3** but not at the conventional 5% level for Partial/Full. R59 adds original actor-linked section-frequency preservation under a within-group permutation comparator; it does not invent the observation that prior experimental segments matter.

## 6. Limitations and reason to keep the mechanism claim on HOLD

1. **One-step versus closed-loop is not a contradiction.** These are different loss functions and induced history distributions; Ross et al. (2011) already warn that a policy's choices alter its future input distribution. EWA/SSM were originally fit partly to simulation moments, not these exact closed-loop conditional MSE criteria.
2. **No causal policy welfare identified.** Each treatment arm is a historically distinct experiment condition, and information treatment changes between repeated segments. Within-session spillovers, assignment/ordering and carryover need their own design analysis. Resampling intervals are descriptive and not guaranteed population inference.
3. **No fixed subject-type identification.** Actor switch-rate correlation across segments supports *persistent predictability*, but both permanent type and learned state may cause it. A dynamic latent-state model with explicit rival restrictions is a candidate for R60, not a result.
4. **Calibration is source-conditional.** All primary results use an actual **exogenous Value tape from heldout subjects** after the decision; using new discrete-uniform Value shocks and an independent seed are *sensitivity experiments*. Neither turns the 24 real sessions into 24 independent samples of causal policy effects.
5. **Cohort exclusion cannot be flattened.** The un-enriched raw file has an extra 20-period pilot session missing from the enriched source. A 40-period simulator may not silently treat all author source files as the same population. The `0 data preparation.R` script's different session exclusion rules noted in R54 remain in force.
6. **State reconstruction is conditional.** Original users may recall 20 practice rounds and prior 40-period sections; the core free-running simulation starts each section with author-rule prior `N=1,A=1` or population-fit history rather than modeling prior experiences. It is a falsification of that *specified transport*, not a complete psychological theory of the historical humans.

## 8. Next R60 scientific decision

**Proposed R60:** *Actor-State Persistence versus Learning Carryover — Group-Matched Cross-Segment Transport and Latent-Mechanism Falsifiers*. First: fit a permanent actor-level risk and a section-updated experience-risk model on earlier segments **without using later segments' outcomes**, then test segment-to-segment transition predictions in the heldout **same sessions**. Preserve randomized treatment sequence and all learning/feedback information filters. Add an exact off-history equivalence test and negative controls for group-shared risk.
