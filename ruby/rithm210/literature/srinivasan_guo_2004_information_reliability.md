# Srinivasan & Guo (2004) — Day-to-Day Evolution of Network Flows Under Route-Choice Dynamics in Commuter Decisions

**Original directly read:** [Research OS Drive](https://drive.google.com/file/d/1x_zH2w3xQlzuiv_zrtYhfuwxDb19ILck/view). Published in Transportation Research Record 1894. Original article studies route choice under day-to-day adjustment, with information attributes and ATIS penetration.

## Key result actually supported
The study uses a traffic simulation framework in which user information, within-day flow patterns and day-to-day adaptation interact. Its results indicate repeated network flows can remain variable even with fixed baseline OD inputs; real-time information can improve or worsen reliability and lead to a **tradeoff between travel time performance and stability**. This is prior art for the claim that 'better information necessarily yields lower congestion variance' being false, and for policy tradeoffs between mean travel time and network volatility.

## Applicability boundary
Their simulation concerns multi-vehicle traffic and ATIS service characteristics, not exact six-person route-choice data nor individual gross U,D event distributions. Model assumptions for who observes information and how choice habits update must be separately checked before numeric transport.

## Next research contrast
To move beyond a qualitative stability-efficiency tradeoff, derive a quantitative path-level welfare bound or testable impulse-response curve that changes in a distinguishable manner when (i) information accuracy, (ii) decision activation correlation, and (iii) gross-turnover cancellation are varied separately. Do not assert a socially priced switching friction from laboratory congestion-cost columns alone.
