# P3-R9 Literature Note B — Kandori, Mailath & Rob (1993)

**Primary file:** [Learning, Mutation, and Long Run Equilibria in Games, Econometrica 61(1), 29–56](https://drive.google.com/file/d/1DIbSzclrlTGoUQR86uRAR0fBzhwz0HI3/view). Original verified by title/abstract and 1993 Econometrica issue.

## Model and theorem scope
A **finite population** has strategies evolving through a success-monotone inertial/myopic dynamic plus persistent random mutation. The small mutation rate is not a one-time shock; it operates each period. The paper characterizes long-run equilibrium selection from the limit of the stationary distribution as mutation probability approaches zero. In two-action symmetric coordination games, the selected outcome is tied to risk dominance rather than merely the Pareto optimum.

## RITHM research boundary
The 24 original six-person sessions exhibit 39 one-step transitions per treatment section. Finite-history P(no switches | current n=4, early/late) cannot be equated with an **epsilon-to-zero stationary distribution**. The original congestion game also has an endogenous opponent-dependent cost difference and may not satisfy the exact pairwise symmetric coordination assumptions of any given risk-dominance proposition.

## Strong alternative to a new theory
Population mutation and Markov resistance may explain persistence/exit hazard without new preferences. First define the actual transition kernel at a fixed N and baseline mutation rate, then test whether its conditional exit times and congestion welfare jointly match observed outcomes. If not, a time-varying learning rule needs an independent information-side implication, not simply an additional inertia parameter.
