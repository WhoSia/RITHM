# Heinrich et al. (2023) — Best-Response Dynamics, Playing Sequences, and Convergence to Equilibrium in Random Games

**Original verified:** [Drive PDF](https://drive.google.com/file/d/1JIN6h5tQf75Y-e4HENYpgHEccml83-2F/view), *International Journal of Game Theory* 52, 703–735, DOI 10.1007/s00182-023-00837-4. Authors Heinrich, Jang, Mungo, Pangallo, Scott, Tarbush & Wiese.

## What is actually compared
The sampling unit is a randomly drawn NORMAL-FORM GAME from a large game ensemble. The paper contrasts a deterministic cyclic player-updating sequence and random player selection, and examines the prevalence of convergence to a pure Nash equilibrium conditional on a pure Nash existing. Theorem 1 treats asymptotic behavior of deterministic playing-sequence best-response in random games; related propositions cover random sequences and game-ensemble limits.

## Crucial assumption distinction
Within a standard finite exact-potential game, STRICT sequential best response has a finite improving path regardless of the scheduling of active players (subject to fair activation). The present RITHM toy's 0↔6 cycle instead results from **simultaneous stale-snapshot updates** by all agents: it is NOT a sequential fixed cyclic order of one agent per stage. Therefore the paper is a strong prior-art barrier for playing-order effects, but its random-game asymptotic statement cannot simply be substituted as a proof of our six-agent model's rational 12/[5(1-s)^2] absorption singularity.
