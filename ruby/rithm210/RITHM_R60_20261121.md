# RITHM-2.10 P3-R60 — Actor-State Persistence versus Learning Carryover

**2026-11-21 KST · Original author-approved 24-session retrospective cohort. Status: predictive evidence, causal mechanism HOLD.** This research unit continues R59's participant-identity linkage by asking whether a person's 39 decisions in the previous experimental section can predict the same person's 39 subsequent switches more accurately than the switching behavior of the other five members of the same group, and how this advantage changes as new evidence accumulates in the target section.

## 1. Information and evaluation contract

The private original source is exactly the author-approved Noussair–Qiao ZIP, SHA-256 `e6adce2da4d1c01455e912aa15144f192108394cbec0bd7f767e2732dcec9953`. We invoke the prior R59 reader, which invokes the R54 byte seal, actor roster, `NumSwitch` parity, and 24-session original population gate **before** processing actor IDs. Retain sections 2, 3 and 4 of 24 sessions with original treatment labels 1..3. Only transitions **into** section 3 from section 2 and **into** section 4 from section 3 enter the new cross-section forecast: **576 linked actor-section pairs × 39 targets = 22,464 individual decisions; 96 six-person group-sections × 39 = 3,744 group transitions**. Previous section's 39 change indicators and known information treatment occur before each target decision. The target person’s own last choice, current previous-section info status, and *already observed* changes within the new section may enter its forecast. The *current* choice, current-period other-person choices, current group occupancy, and current-period payoff never enter a forecast before scoring. The estimated population propensity is learned on **the other 23 sessions** for each heldout session. Group-level (`U,D`) distribution is the convolution of six individual probabilities; no serial within-period action order is invented. The current (target) section's first route is conditioned upon, not forecast.

All numerical comparisons are **descriptive on the same 24 repeatedly examined sessions**, not preregistered independent confirmation. The shrunk prior exposure is **20 pseudo-observations** (retained from the preceding R57 development), with sensitivities 5 and 80. A person may have memory of a preceding section, but this analysis uses an **analyst-available, temporally legal** history and does not assert the experiment explicitly displayed all previous-section history on the target screen. Participant information masks and treatment context are preserved; group-wide occupancy is not used as a No-information actor's predictor.

For a train-only baseline `p0(treatment, informed, previous_route)` (Jeffreys smoothing) and prior section observed gross switch count `K_i∈[0,39]`, current observed-prefix switch count `J_i(t)∈[0,t−2]`, and other five previous section gross switch count `K_{−i}∈[0,195]`, compare:

- **Base:** `p0`.
- **Other-five prior:** `(K_{−i}+20p0)/(195+20)`; this expressly excludes the focal actor.
- **Own 39-step prior:** `(K_i+20p0)/(39+20)`.
- **Current-only:** `(J_i(t)+20p0)/(t−2+20)`.
- **Carryover + current:** `(K_i+J_i(t)+20p0)/(39+(t−2)+20)`.
- **Previous ten only:** `K_i` restricted to prior-section final 10 changes, `(K_i^{10}+20p0)/(10+20)`.

This is an explicitly specified **beta-Bernoulli empirical predictive family**, not an exact implementation of EWA-lite, a structural theory of memory, or a measurement of psychological cost.

## 2. Original human source-derived matched-fold scores

All scores are **equal weight per heldout session** (sessions, not actor-periods, are the source clusters). Lower natural-log loss is better:

| Model | Actor next-switch logloss | Group joint `(U,D)` logloss |
|---|---:|---:|
| Train-only treatment + info + own route | **0.547181** | **2.034749** |
| Previous-section other five participants | 0.571964 | 2.112471 |
| **Previous-section same individual** | **0.518122** | **1.996528** |
| Current section history only | **0.504398** | **1.939944** |
| Previous individual + current history | **0.503220** | 1.959185 |
| Previous-section last ten choices | 0.526939 | 1.991286 |

Previous-person vs base change on actor logloss: **−0.029059** with whole-session descriptive resampling **[−0.041544,−0.016709]**. Previous-group-other vs base: **+0.024782** [**+0.013426,+0.034828**]. Thus not just a high/low-movement *group* proxy; individual history carries predictive signal even when the previous section's other five are available.

However, compared with current-only, adding preceding individual history improves overall actor logloss by merely **−0.001178** [**−0.005763,+0.004121**], and the group joint loss is **+0.019242** [**−0.003981,+0.042970**]. Do **not** call a decisive overall benefit of carrying the old section's propensity forward.

### Time-dependent transport tension

The following segments were examined **exploratorily and post hoc**, not prespecified in an independent holdout:

| Target time in new section | Base | Current-only | Previous + current | Previous+current minus current-only |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 2–6 (5 target decisions) | 0.56424 | 0.55737 | **0.53176** | **−0.025611** [−0.038812, −0.012201] |
| 7–20 | 0.55786 | 0.51924 | 0.51452 | −0.00472 (point only) |
| 21–40 | 0.53544 | **0.48077** | 0.48817 | **+0.007405** [+0.002993, +0.012010] |

**Interpretation:** previous-section behavior can initialize a useful personal forecast at the start of the next treatment. Once the new treatment has accumulated many observations, a fixed 39-observation weight on the previous environment may hinder both individual and group forecasts. This is evidence for **prediction-context dependence**, NOT identification of memory decay or a proof that a person intentionally discards an old habit. It may reflect baseline hazard changes and old/new treatment nonexchangeability. All section boundaries change treatment; on exactly 2/3 of actor-links the informed flag changes. Source-split by changed versus unchanged information masks preserves the broad prior-vs-current pattern, without providing a causal instrument.

### Original-person negative control

Use the source's **96 target six-person group-sections**, retaining each group's current individual choices, target treatment, and fixed training-fitted baseline while **reassigning only previous-section propensity estimates**. Make 2,000 independently seeded permutations of the identity assignment within each group. Restrict more tightly to matching previous-and-current information status; the strictest test additionally matches **previous section's final route and target section's first route**. For the prior-own-only model the pooled (not session-equal) *actual* actor logloss is **0.517170**:

| Exchangeability reference | Eligible actor-links | Null mean actor logloss | Null 95% range | Null outcomes ≤ actual |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Within six-person group | 576 | 0.572892 | [0.564184, 0.581320] | 0/2,000 |
| Within same group + both information masks | 576 | 0.565876 | [0.556825, 0.574617] | 0/2,000 |
| Group + both information masks + two boundary routes | **354** (in 93/96 groups) | **0.542365** | **[0.535637,0.549100]** | **0/2,000** |

This shows source-linked personal history survives a stringent label-reassignment diagnostic; the continuity-corrected *reference* probability is at most **1/2,001 ≈ 0.00050** under that **specific within-stratum exchangeable-label null**, not a population p-value for memory causation or stable personality. In the strict test only 354/576 actors are permutable; others remain identical, and this is not a test of universal identity exchangeability.

## 3. Exact mechanism rival on two observed section summaries

Consider two binary per-section summary indicators `X1,X2`. In a **permanent latent type** world, draw H∈{low,high} fairly; conditional on H, the two summaries are independent Bernoulli with `p_low=1/5`, `p_high=4/5`. In a **direct experience-response** world, draw X1 fairly, then X2 copies X1 with probability 17/25 (otherwise inverts). Both induce **exactly** `P(0,0)=P(1,1)=17/50`, `P(0,1)=P(1,0)=4/25`. The complete two-summary observable law is equal despite distinct mechanisms. This exact result uses Python `Fraction` with a source-free test. **It does not assert equivalence of the complete within-section human choice trajectories, of the original 40-period game, or of a third experimental segment**. The additional human detail might distinguish stronger versions of these competing mechanisms, but the present scores do not identify the causal mechanism.

## 4. RITHM-2.10 program position, prior art and next discriminator

- R2.10's starting P0 exact common-shock covariance `a(6−a)δ²` already showed joint adjustment correlations are insufficient for strategic coordination. R55's sharp occupancy-fiber theorem shows occupancy misses gross churn. R57–R60 now demonstrate identity and previous-exposure predictive persistence under increasingly stringent source-level tests.
- The original Noussair–Qiao *Congestion Information and Efficiency: An Experiment* Appendix E already investigates **order/carryover effects** in its sections and finds them prominently for No-information section 3. We claim neither an original discovery of carryover nor that the current person-memory model is its true explanation.
- Existing sequential model exposure-bias work including **Talvitie (2014), _Model Regularization for Stable Sample Rollouts_** (already held in Drive) preempts generic novelty for the R59 closed-loop issue. R60's contribution is the particular matched human identity transfer audit and contrast against group-peer and within-current alternatives, not a new general learning theorem.
- **Priority R61 falsifier:** match *within-person stable rate* and *lagged received reward/experienced information* as explicit separate structural rival states across the same section boundaries. Require train-only nested selection of the prior/online weighting (not selecting a time window after reading test results), held-session calibration, direct next-group `(U,D)` scoring and a fully autonomous welfare sensitivity check. No new participant experiment is required, but none of these steps is yet evidence of causal payoff learning.

**HOLD:** human cognitive inertia as a causal mechanism; permanent person types; post-treatment information intervention policy effect; real switch resource price κ; external population inference; publication-level novelty; transport of one-seat-per-period synthetic welfare results to simultaneous original human choices.
