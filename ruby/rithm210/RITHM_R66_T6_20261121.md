## 1. Scientific question: what does the second-order covariance audit miss?

At previous A occupancy \(n_A=4\) in a six-person two-route game, original-paper route costs \(c_A(n_A)=n_A\) and \(c_B(n_B)=2n_B\) give total congestion

\[
C(n_A)=n_A^2+2(6-n_A)^2=24+3(n_A-4)^2.
\]

For independent or dependent simultaneous decisions \(X_i\in\{0,1\}\), signed next-state occupancy is \(n_A'=n_A+\sum_i s_i X_i\), \(s_i=+1\) for B→A and \(s_i=-1\) for A→B. **One-period quadratic social cost uses only the first two moments of \(X\)**; individual marginal switch probabilities and all pairwise covariance terms determine its expectation exactly, irrespective of any third and higher-order dependence.

However, the next *occupancy distribution* is not determined by those moments. Later actors respond to the new state; the finite-horizon value function need not be quadratic. Consequently the R66-T2 observed signed covariance formula is a one-step identity, **not a sufficient statistic for long-horizon welfare**.

## 2. Exact three-actor parity witness and its economic meaning

Initial six-agent allocation \(n_A=4\); three of the four A actors can choose A→B, two old-B actors and the fourth old-A actor do not change at this first decision. Introduce two joint laws over the three indicator bits:

| Joint law | Four equally likely 3-bit vectors |
|---|---|
| Even parity | 000, 011, 101, 110 |
| Odd parity | 001, 010, 100, 111 |

All individual \(P(X_i=1)=1/2\). All pairwise \(P(X_i=X_j=1)=1/4\), hence **every pairwise covariance is zero**. Expected initial total switches \(E[K]=3/2\) and expected first post-transition excess cost \(3E[K^2]=9\) are identical. But \(E[X_1X_2X_3]\) is zero under even parity and 1/4 under odd parity, producing distinct occupancy distributions after the first action:

* even: A-occupancy 4 with probability 1/4; 2 with probability 3/4;
* odd: A-occupancy 3 with probability 3/4; 1 with probability 1/4.

From this point forward impose exactly the **same synthetic full-public-feedback rule** under both interventions: when \(n_A<4\), precisely one B actor moves to A per period, and once \(n_A=4\), everyone stays. This rule is deliberately imposed and is not inferred to describe original human behavior.

With \(K\in\{0,1,2,3\}\) first-period A→B departures, after the next step the system has excess cost \(3K^2\). It requires K subsequent deterministic repair steps to return to the optimum, creating exact long-horizon excess

\[
F(K)=3\sum_{j=1}^Kj^2
=\frac{K(K+1)(2K+1)}2
=K^3+\frac32K^2+\frac12K.
\]

A QUADRATIC single-period congestion externality has become a **CUBIC cumulative burden** because the return time depends on how far the system deviates. Therefore, for the even and odd laws whose first and pairwise moments coincide,

\[
\Delta J_{H\ge4}=
\Delta E[K^3]
=6\Delta E[X_1X_2X_3]
=6(1/4)=\boxed{3/2}.
\]

Exact 40-period accounting from \(n_A=4\): even cumulative excess \(45/4=11.25\), odd \(51/4=12.75\). Both incur **exactly 3 expected gross route changes across the entire horizon**, including their initial departures and later return transitions. For any identical nonnegative physical resource fee \(f\) per route change, the difference remains \(3/2\). This is not a generic welfare ranking of synchronized versus independent revisions; in fact each law is PAIRWISE independent, with a different THIRD-order dependence.

The parity construction is standard elementary probability (not new). The 6-person congestion/recovery example is an explicitly conditional applied counterexample; its novelty relative to the literature has not been established and no claim about real human parity coordination is made.

## 3. General finite-horizon propagation identity

For any two row-stochastic transition kernels \(P,Q\), row starting distribution \(\mu_0\), state-period social reward/cost vector \(\ell\), and horizon H,

\[
J_H(P)=\sum_{t=0}^{H-1}\mu_0 P^t\ell.
\]

The exact (NOT first-order Taylor) difference is

\[
\boxed{J_H(Q)-J_H(P)=
\sum_{k=0}^{H-2}\mu_0 Q^k(Q-P)
\left[\sum_{j=0}^{H-2-k}P^j\ell\right].}
\]

This is the standard telescoping identity \(Q^t-P^t=\sum_{k=0}^{t-1}Q^k(Q-P)P^{t-1-k}\), organized into continuation values. It identifies when a one-step difference in joint transition probabilities enters later state rewards. In the parity construction, the initial post-action distributions have equal quadratic excess cost but their dot products with the continuation value differ.
