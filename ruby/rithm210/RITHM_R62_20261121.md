# RITHM-2.10 P3-R62 — Collective Stability, Legal Marginal Repair & the Limits of Latent Group-State Claims

**2026-11-21 KST · RITHM-2.10 OPEN · original-author-approved retrospective experiment; no new recruitment.**

## Research-director verdict

**An apparent collective coordination residual can be dominated by a misspecified independent individual model.** The R61 autonomous model fails badly on efficient A=4 allocation persistence, but the first priority is not to add common shocks. A researcher-visible previous-occupancy correction alone brings one-step joint-flow logloss from **1.939944 to 1.906863**; more importantly, adding only legitimately **personally received prior payoff and self-route tenure** to an independent model reduces it to **1.875827** with R61 current history, or **1.865735** with R61's train-selected memory discount. The added all-six-stay latent gate is then **not selected in any of 24 holdout folds** if calibrated to overall joint flow logloss. A low-dimensional symmetric shared shock reduces logloss only by **−0.001034**, with whole-session resampling interval **[−0.002514,+0.000386]** including zero. **No causal evidence of coordination or collective psychological synchrony is established.**

The personal-feedback independent model also markedly improves 40-period autonomous group-total congestion MSE from **7,157.902** (R61 current-only) to **2,872.950** in the observed-initial 96-section cohort. An additional A4 all-stay gate lowers its MSE to **2,797.367**, but its paired 24-session interval versus the legally informed independent model **[−167.667,+19.650]** includes zero; a second simulation seed changes the modest shared-shock ordering. After the large gain from legal personal feedback, the remaining collective-gate gain is at most **exploratory**, not an identified new mechanism or guaranteed policy benefit. Even the stronger model still underpredicts human A4 persistence and does not recover the treatment-specific long-run dynamics.

## 2. Formal marginal-preserving collective-pause counterexample

For six pre-decision flip probabilities \(p_i\in(0,1)\) and a common latent pause probability \(0\le q<1-\max_i p_i\), set all \(X_i=0\) with probability q and, otherwise, draw independent \(X_i\sim\mathrm{Bernoulli}[p_i/(1-q)]\). Then **each** \(E[X_i]=p_i\) exactly, while

\[
P(G=0)=q+(1-q)\prod_{i=1}^{6}\left(1-\frac{p_i}{1-q}\right).
\]

For \(i\ne j\), the simultaneous-switch covariance is **\(\operatorname{Cov}(X_i,X_j)=q p_i p_j/(1-q)\)**. Writing \(s_i=+1\) for a previous B user and \(-1\) for a previous A user, the one-period expected total congestion difference from the individual independent model is exactly

\[
\Delta E[C]=\frac{6q}{1-q}\sum_{i<j}s_is_jp_ip_j,
\quad C(n_A)=24+3(n_A-4)^2.
\]

Thus positive individual-switch correlation can **increase or decrease expected congestion**, depending on the signs of the previous routes. An exact rational six-actor witness with \(p_i=1/4\) for all actors, old A occupancy four and q=1/5 has **\(\Delta E[C]=-3/32\)**, whereas other previous occupancies yield positive values with the same marginal p's. The independent target law and the latent law are calculated by full 64-mask enumeration; the accelerated batch implementation matches the scalar calculation and no-switch law on synthetic cases. **This elementary construction is not a novel theorem or a fit to human causation.** It is a finite negative-control model.

## 3. R61's A4 mismatch has a marginal calibration component

Across the 1,569 original t−1 A=4 group transitions in sections 3–4:

| Metric | Actual humans | R61 current-only independent |
|---|---:|---:|
| Gross person changes per A4 transition | **1.12173** | 1.46646 predicted |
| P(no person changes given previous A4) | **0.32760** | 0.18702 predicted |
| Last t≥21 A4 gross switches | 1.02238 | 1.43849 predicted |

This is **not** evidence that covariance is needed: the independent actor marginals themselves overpredict departures from observed A4 states. The converse non-A4 group transitions have actual gross **1.76920** versus predicted **1.56439**, so a single global propensity correction is inadequate. Stronger competitors must be confronted before accepting a common-state explanation.

Two-segment subgroup models use *one* designated outer heldout original session and 23 source training sessions. The stronger **researcher-oracle** propensity adds an offset \(\mathrm{logit}(p'_i)=\mathrm{logit}(p_i)+\delta_{\mathbf 1[n_A=4]}\) whose strata are fitted on 23 other sessions; an epoch interaction provides a further two intercepts. This model leaves actor switches conditionally independent, yet gives joint \((U,D)\) logloss **1.906863** for A4/non-A4 or **1.906401** for A4×early/late. It must **not** be represented as a law an uninformed person could implement.

## 5. Strongest predecision-legal individual-information challenger

An outer heldout session contributes **no current or future outcome to training**, and `Value_t` from the original CSV is never an actor predictor. ## 6. 40-period autonomous transport, no teacher-forcing

All six people choose simultaneously. The period-1 actor route is fixed from the historical group in the primary run; there is a separate **fair 50:50 first-route** sensitivity. From period 2 on, all generated choices update the actors' own streak and prior route, and route occupancy determines the realized cost. Original period-t individual **exogenous Value tape** is read exclusively **after the simulated period-t choice** to generate each actor's *realized* simulated payoff; no future value, unchosen payoff or other actors' occupancy appears as that actor's decision input. There are **96 source group-sections, 64 Monte-Carlo repetitions per group, seed 20261121**, with same random uniforms across model variants. The group-total 40-period excess congestion MSE is evaluated against the *actual historical total per group*, rather than assuming actual policy welfare can be estimated.

| Generated model, actual first route fixed | Group-total excess cost MSE | A4 occupancy share | P(no-switch|previous A4) |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| R61 current-only independent | **7157.902** | .3129 | .1749 |
| **Legal self-payoff/streak independent** | **2872.950** | .349 | .195 |
| Legal independent + A4 pause, q=.10 | 2797.367 | .364 | .243 |
| Legal independent + symmetric shared shock | 2818.649 | .348 | .208 |
| Historical human behavior | Not model MSE | **.4203** | **.3276** |

The legal self-feedback model's change relative to R61 is **−4284.95 group-level MSE** (paired whole-session resampling, as R61−legal **[+3361.46,+5226.38]**). However, extra A4 pause versus the **proper strong legal independent** model is only **−75.58** with cluster-resampling **[−167.67,+19.65]**, including zero. The shared-shock gain is **−54.30** [−219.42,+91.45], also inconclusive.

**Sensitivity:** fair first routes yield R61 **8222.39**, legal independent **3901.64**, legal A4 pause **3648.94**, legal common shock **3904.88**. Independent seed **20261123** under actual first routes: legal independent **2833.72**, A4 pause **2806.47**, common shock **2997.02**. The shared-shock advantage even changes sign under a different Monte-Carlo seed, so it is not an established macro improvement. Correctly accounting for an unknown external per-switch resource price \(\kappa\) requires the separate error polynomial \(\mathrm{MSE}(\kappa)=A+B\kappa+D\kappa^2\); the coefficient triplets are archived per model. \(\kappa\) is not observed, estimated or recommended.

**Major remaining failures:** Even the stronger legal A4-pause rollout predicts an efficient A4 state only ~36.4% (historical 42.0%), and P0 given previous A4 only ~24.3% (historical 32.8%). The source Full−No total cost difference on these target sections is **−1.875**, but the legal independent generated contrast is **+6.64** (from ~155.61 vs ~148.97). These contrasts have no identified causal meaning, and a pooled macro fit does not repair treatment differences. Improving 40-round total group cost is not equivalent to correctly reproducing every target.

## 7. Evidence, prior art and protection against overclaims

- Primary original experiment: Noussair & Qiao, [*Congestion Information and Efficiency: An Experiment*, *Management Science* 72(7), 2026, 6229–6250](https://doi.org/10.1287/mnsc.2023.00056); user's original [author manuscript](https://drive.google.com/file/d/1rTL9qTpYV6zwee6pC3nZy64H07oSWzlK/view) and [online Appendix A/B/E](https://drive.google.com/file/d/1xwIj82h_LTrgffv2w4XtJs2KDc8980RW/view) are separate versions. Original authors already model information-dependent route choice; this research compares alternative *forecast functions*, not previously unknown experimental facts.
- Correlated binary observations, shared group heterogeneity and extra all-zero components have substantial prior art: [Markov counting models for correlated binary responses](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5963474/); [Cantarella–Watling (2016), stochastic day-to-day assignment and stability](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0191261516302776). The shared-pause construction is **not a new general mathematical result**.
- **Repeated-exploration constraint:** RITHM R23–R61 have repeatedly used this same original source. Whole-session resampling intervals are exploratory and cannot repair repeated model-selection exposure, represent external preregistration, or identify real psychological inertia/cooperation.
- **Information-lawful versus analyst-oracle:** `r62_statecal.py` is not a No-information subject cognitive model. Any common latent state conditioned on exact A occupancy is an analyst's joint-state predictive distribution, not proof that subjects observed such occupancy or communicated.
- **Distinct scoring targets:** 3,744 one-step group outcomes are not 96 self-generated 40-step group forecasts. One-step grouped logloss, A4 no-switch Brier, gross switching, long-term congestion MSE and treatment differences need separate labels. Some may improve while others worsen.
- **Privacy and CI boundary:** All source human actor/route/Value details remain in the owner-authorized private ZIP; public GitHub/Drive reproducibility ZIP contains **code, synthetic tests and aggregate-only results**. GitHub Actions is strictly **source-free**; local original replay is a separate real-data verification.
