The 2–6-switch breakdown is 1,592 / 853 / 276 / 49 / 6 transitions, respectively, for 8,909 observed gross individual route switches across all transitions. **The 788 constant-occupancy churn transitions account for 1,734 gross individual route switches; 840 of those are within the 385 observed 4:2→4:2 churn transitions.** These route changes are not recoverable from occupancy differences alone. If a separately justified per-switch resource price `κ` were added, the corresponding omitted term for these same-occupancy transitions would be `1,734κ`; the experiment does not by itself estimate `κ`. Every one of the 24 retained sessions contains at least one period with multiple actor changes. The source contains *period-boundary choices*, not recorded within-period microaction order. Thus one-person-per-period R49–R53 dynamics **do not describe this original timing law**. Relabeling observed gross change as an arbitrarily ordered asynchronous sequence would change the unit of observation and expose nonexistent subperiod state measurements.

The observed per-group period-end congestion cost remains **fully measurable from its boundary occupancy** via 

`C(n) = n*n + 2*(6-n)*(6-n) = 24 + 3*(n-4)^2`.

In particular, equal final occupancy does not mean that nobody moved: those 788 periods include gross churn not detected by occupancy changes alone. An added resource cost per actual switch would depend on a separately justified unit parameter, but the count of switches is observable and need not be inferred from occupancy.

## 3. Original-data-based exact hidden-order falsifier

Consider the 173 observed transitions with A occupancy 5 both before and after the period and two distinct individuals changing in opposite directions. Let one move from B to A, the other from A to B. The source records both changed identities but **does not record which revision happened first**.

Under the *strictly hypothetical* minimal-microaction convention (each net-changing person changes exactly once, one person at a time, and after each microaction excess congestion is priced):

- A→B first: occupancies `5 → 4 → 5`, microaction excess scores `0 + 3 = 3`;
- B→A first: occupancies `5 → 6 → 5`, microaction excess scores `12 + 3 = 15`.

Both action orders have the same full actor-level **observed beginning and ending vectors**. The possible microaction scores are different. Hence micro-order is not identified by the published period-level source, even when identities and individual switch indicators are known. **This does not imply that the actual historical experiment charged those microaction scores; it did not observe these intermediate states.** The actual period-end congestion cost is 3 excess points in both worlds, independent of that order.

For all six-person transitions, an exact dynamic program over remaining A→B and B→A movers finds the minimum and maximum sum of hypothetical post-microaction excess scores. In 1,196/5,616 transitions (21.30%), these extremes differ; among the 2,776 transitions with at least two changers, 43.08% have multiple micro-order costs. Even restricting to the **minimum number of changes compatible with the endpoints**, period-boundary data cannot choose between those paths. Because independent ordering is permitted by construction, summing their minima and maxima gives an illustrative global **hypothetical microstep score** range `[22,842,45,126]`. This number is NOT the observed historical social cost and must never be labeled as such.

## 5. Research judgment and next falsifier

The key substantive lesson is a **transport obstruction**, not evidence that the original experiment had sequentially priced inner microactions. R53's finite-clock partial realized-welfare ranking theorem is exact under its own one-seat-per-period assumptions but **cannot be applied directly to the original 24-session simultaneous period choices**. To connect human evidence to original goals, use the recorded *joint transition* of all six actors between two periods. Observable quantities are the occupancy after each period, all six route-change indicators, gross churn, and observed period-end congestion; the unobserved quantities are inner chronological choice order, response timing within a period, and any hypothetical inner costs.

Next R55 priority: identify distinct joint six-actor transition laws that agree on occupancy transitions but disagree on gross movement costs, and identify what full actor-level changes distinguish. Separately, preserve R39–R41 original inertia/coordination comparisons and session-level folds; no claims about causal strategic coordination from raw churn correlations. **No new human recruitment; no original participant rows in GitHub.**
