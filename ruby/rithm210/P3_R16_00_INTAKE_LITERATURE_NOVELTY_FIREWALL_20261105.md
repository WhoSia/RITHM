## B. What the papers ALREADY establish
**Cantarella & Watling (2016), primarily sections 3–5:** a general day-to-day stochastic process with route choice conditional on remembered past costs, habit / inertia from exponential smoothing, finite-memory and infinite-memory cost filters; mean-process deterministic approximation, stationary distribution arguments under regular random-utility assumptions, asymptotic scaling, and dissipativity / stability conditions. Section 4 explicitly assumes independent choices conditional on remembered past states and notes unconditional dependence may arise through congestion. This paper directly preempts claiming novelty for independent microswitches + shared congestion feedback + historical memory + stochastic stationary dynamics.

**Qi et al. (2024), sections 2–6:** route-dependent attraction (preference) and route-dependent inertia from Part I are embedded in a stochastic day-to-day route choice model. Conditional independent Bernoulli users yield Binomial route-by-route flows and Poisson-binomial overall occupancy. Model validates switching flow, one-step occupancy distribution, equilibrium flow distribution and tests generalization. Authors report a 312-participant, eight-scenario lab experiment, 75–110 rounds and full route feedback after choice (their study's protocol, not identical to the Noussair-Qiao Full/Partial treatment). The paper offers an approximate equilibrium distribution model, stability analysis, and transport policy applications. This directly preempts presenting a stochastic independent-agent occupancy Markov chain as novel.

**Wu, Yin, Lynch & Liu (2026)**, *Statistical Inference of Day-to-Day Traffic Dynamics*, available in Drive as arXiv:2605.02806v2 [PDF](https://drive.google.com/file/d/1LQON5ENZefmWAuMgwy6r9s4xv7GytDsv/view), also materially competes with RITHM's identification program: formal identifiability and consistency of Bayesian inference for an individual stochastic adjustment model; hierarchical heterogeneity, anonymous-observation model, lab and real-road trajectory data. The article explicitly takes the daily route-cost vector as an exogenously given model input (its section 2.1), so identification of that behavioral family is logically different from identifying endogenous, intervention-specific congestion welfare. This does NOT mean our alternative has yet been proved original.

## D. Exact finite-state thought experiment: observability gate
N agents, current occupancy a, next occupancy b, U=B->A, D=A->B, b=a+U-D. Feasible D runs from max(0,a-b) to min(a,N-b). Gross G=U+D hence attains ALL numbers of correct parity between
`|b-a| <= G <= min(a+b, 2N-a-b)`.
This is a SHARP elementary combinatorial bound, no uniqueness or newness claim. With 6 agents, a=b=4, G can be 0, 2 or 4 while next congestion C(4)=24 stays the same. If switching causes actual marginal resource loss k>0, its aggregate realized cost is not identified by occupancy alone. Its exact path bounds add over consecutive occupancy transitions; hypothetical k is NOT observed psychological friction. New generic N<=8 exhaustive enumerator locally passed **4 tests / 1144 assertions / 0 failures**.

Sharper same-marginal counterexample ([exact Rational Ruby witness](p3/turnover_coupling_witness.rb)): start with A actors 0,1,2,3 and B actors 4,5. Only 0,1,4,5 are allowed to switch, each with marginal p=.5, other two p=0. Coupling SHOCK chooses G=0 with probability 1/2 or all four eligible actors switching (G=4) with probability 1/2. Coupling COMPENSATED always switches exactly one eligible A and one eligible B (G=2), uniformly over the four pairs. Both mechanisms give identical six individual switch marginals, deterministic next occupancy 4, and E[G]=2. But Var(G) is **4 vs 0**, and P(G>=4) **1/2 vs 0**. In fact equal marginal switch probabilities IDENTIFY E[G] and thus expected *linear* per-switch resource cost; what is NOT identified is gross-switch distribution, its tails, and convex coordination overhead such as E[G²] (**8 vs 4**). No causal claim follows from this constructive example; both joint couplings are simply possible distributions. Local synthetic tests: **3 tests / 17 assertions / 0 failures**. All constants exact Rationals.
