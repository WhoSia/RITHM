# RITHM-2.10 P3-R61 — Nested Memory Discounting, Identity-Specific Transport & Closed-Loop Stability Failure

**2026-11-21 · original-author-approved human source · retrospective discovery audit · NOT a new human study or causal welfare result**

## Research-director verdict

**The gain from a train-only, fast-discounted personal history is real on the examined 24-session heldout one-period individual prediction score, but it fails as a stand-alone autonomous 40-period congestion model.** An actor's preceding experimental section is a useful prior during the early target periods. Its impact should be tempered as actual behavior under the newly assigned information environment is observed. Across independent heldout sessions, a nested training-only exponential discount improves actor logloss relative to both no earlier history and equally weighted permanent earlier history. However, in autonomous six-person simulation, the same adaptive model predicts a substantially **worse group-level 40-period congestion total** than the model using only the current section's own choices. Both underestimate the actual later capacity to stabilize at the efficient 4:2 allocation.

This is a *predictive and falsification* result. We have **not** identified psychological decay of memory, stable subject type, group coordination, a causal payoff-learning channel, real switching cost κ, or an information-policy welfare effect. We also have re-examined these same 24 sessions and several model families many times; none of the intervals is preregistered confirmatory inference.

## 2. Explicit adaptive prior and exact R60 limits

For the individual's previous section: \(y_p\) switches in 39 opportunities. For the target prefix prior to period t: \(y_c\) switches in \(n_c=t−2\) observed opportunities. For the base flip probability \(p_0\), estimated entirely in other original sessions, define:

\[
 p_t(\tau)=\frac{y_c +w_\tau(n_c)y_p+20p_0}{n_c+39 w_\tau(n_c)+20},\qquad
 w_\tau(n)=\begin{cases}0&\tau=0\\e^{-n/\tau}&0<\tau<\infty\\1&\tau=\infty.\end{cases}
\]

Frozen candidate grid: **τ∈{0,2,4,8,16,32,64,∞}**. τ=0 exactly matches R60 `current_only` and τ=∞ exactly matches R60 `own_prior_plus_current`, including the original pseudo-count 20 and base training. The 24 outer fits select **τ=8 in 22 sessions**, **τ=16 in 2 sessions**. Hyperparameter selection fold-by-fold is distinct from fitting a behavioral cognitive-memory mechanism. Each actor's probability is bounded strictly between 0 and 1; the group (U,D) law is the convolution of independent Bernoulli decisions separated by previous A/B route.

The selected τ is fixed before applying the null, and it was selected without held-session data. Permute the source previous-section counts **within their same six-person original group only**. The strictest null matches both old/new informed masks and both preceding terminal and new initial routes. Other actors' traits or future outcomes cannot enter a forecast; they are preserved only to define the *statistical exchangeability null*.

Pooled actual actor logloss **0.495987**; whole-group permutation exact mean **0.508729**, matching informed masks **0.507102**, stricter informed+boundary routes **0.501668**. ## 5. Long-horizon transport audit: individual predictability ≠ group congestion model

Use the exact fitted outer-session base and train-selected τ, then simulate all six actors' **own** target-section route changes simultaneously, updating each model with its own prior simulated switch counts and previous route. At each of 96 groups, average **64 independent Monte-Carlo paths** with identical common uniform draws across models; score the difference between each group's model-mean **40-period cumulative** excess congestion and its observed original cumulative excess congestion. This metric is **group-total forecast MSE**, not one-period logloss or causal welfare. Two initial states are checked: actual t1 route masks (conditional) and fair 50:50 t1 coin flips.

**Where does the simulation fail?** For actual source target sections 3/4, over all 40 periods, efficient A occupancy 4 appears **42.03%** of the time, versus simulated current-only 31.47%, nested 30.56%, static 28.90%. In the last ten periods, efficient occupancy is **47.50%** in human history, vs current-only 29.90%, nested 29.57%. Conditional on a prior efficient 4:2 occupancy, observed **no actor changing while staying 4:2** occurs **32.76%**, vs current-only 17.63%, nested **16.31%**. Observed final-ten share of same effective no-switch is **41.29%** versus nested ~16.19%. Self-generated independent propensity updating **underproduces stable efficient group configurations**, and its simulated mean excess congestion per period rises late even while observed human mean excess declines. The agent-only hazard/short-term memory model thus lacks a needed group stability mechanism, but these totals cannot discriminate between strategic interaction, unobserved group shocks, selective joint learning, or stable heterogeneous actor states.

**Important scope limit:** R61's autonomous sample targets only 96 later-section groups, with different conditioning than R59's earlier **144** total groups and different predictors. Do not compare their numeric MSEs as if they were a single matched tournament. Original practice periods, prior period-specific private payoff dynamics, true hidden cognitive costs and actual congestion-price intervention are not simulated by this R61 family.

## 6. Three segments can distinguish one exact pair of latent competitors

The R60 two-binary-summary observational equivalence is real but not universal. Consider **three** binary observed section summaries. In world T, one hidden propensity p∈{1/5,4/5} is picked once per actor and reused for all three summaries, with independent outcomes conditional on type. In world M, the first summary is fair Bernoulli 1/2 and each later one **copies its immediate predecessor with probability 17/25**. The two specified mechanisms have **exactly the same full law on each adjacent pair** of section summaries: P(00)=P(11)=17/50; P(01)=P(10)=4/25. But for nonadjacent 1st and 3rd summaries, P(equal) is **17/25** under the hidden type and **353/625** under first-order history response. Full triple-law total variation distance is exactly **72/625**. That third observation can thus separate *these two restricted models*, a constructive distinguishing signal beyond the R60 two-summary witness; this is not a new generic theorem.

Using **actual original actors** and three completed experimental sections, 288 participant triples yield switch-rate correlations: section 2→3 **0.58720**, section 3→4 **0.62903**, section 2→4 **0.60973**, 24-session descriptive bootstrap intervals in `r61_three_segment_results.json`. Historical switch *rates* are continuous, not the synthetic binary summaries; crucially, treatment order, information mask and personal experience change across sections. Do **not** equate these correlations to the synthetic type or Markov transition parameters or claim an identified fixed type.
