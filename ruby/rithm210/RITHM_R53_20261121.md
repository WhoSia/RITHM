# RITHM-2.10 · P3-R53 — Exact Cost-Sign Identification from Fixed Revisions

2026-11-21 KST · finite-clock synthetic demonstration, source-free

## 1. Scope and estimand

R51 identified first-switch outcomes only partially when the full future was not observed. R52 showed that the actual scheduled seat order can sharply restrict the remaining 6-person congestion path. R53 asks whether **the sign of a realized difference in mean cumulative costs between groups already assigned the −4 and +4 settlement labels** can be inferred from valid incomplete histories. This realized contrast is **not** the causal effect of the payment sign: the two groups may have different unobserved dispositions, and an assigned-arm empirical difference is not a counterfactual effect without additional assumptions and sampling analysis.

The focal first-switch endpoint and total group cost are distinct functions of the same trajectory. The focal can be never scheduled again, while other actors independently move occupancy towards or away from 4:2. The outcome zero in this special clock therefore does not logically force zero excess congestion or zero difference across groups.

## 2. Proposition: sharp endpoint comparison across independent group completions

Let `G_-` and `G_+` be the sets of distinct assigned groups for each signed settlement, within the same initial occupancy, horizon, and frame. Both must be nonempty. Each group `i` has an audited frozen prefix, starting route mask `m_i`, a scheduled future seat sequence `s_{i,t}`, and a finite set `Ω_i` of physically allowable complete paths. The only possible choices at the scheduled seat are KEEP or FLIP; existing committed events must be preserved. The group cumulative excess or half-point model resource cost is `C_i(ω_i)`.

Set `l_i = min_{Ω_i} C_i` and `u_i = max_{Ω_i} C_i`; R52 computes both exactly and produces witness choices. The realized signed arm contrast is

```text
Δ = mean_{i in G_-} C_i - mean_{j in G_+} C_j .
L = mean_{i in G_-} l_i - mean_{j in G_+} u_j .
U = mean_{i in G_-} u_i - mean_{j in G_+} l_j .
```

Under the **product-completion assumption** that each group's otherwise unrestricted future actions may be chosen independently of other groups, the extreme realized contrasts are exactly `L` and `U`. Proof: any path cost lies between each group's `l_i` and `u_i`; linearity of the two sample averages proves `L ≤ Δ ≤ U`. Combine each minus-arm group's min with each plus-arm group's max to attain `L`, and reverse these choices to attain `U`. All these witnesses preserve the frozen prefix, assigned sign, and individual revision clock. No probability distribution or welfare response model is assumed.

**Sign criteria:** `L>0` means −4 is always more costly; `U<0` always less costly; `L<0<U` establishes that two complete worlds with the same observed prefixes give opposite strict cost rankings. `L=0<U` and `L<U=0` support only weak ranking; `L=U=0` equality. Costs are integer/discrete so the intermediate real numbers between extremes need not all be attainable; R53 claims **sharp extrema and sign attainability**, not that the entire interval is an identified continuum.

**Boundary:** If one requires a common behavioral policy, cross-group latent dependence, exchangeability, or a shared parameter across all groups, then the completion set may not factor into a Cartesian product, so these separate extrema need not be jointly sharp. No causal policy-welfare partial order is claimed. Similarly, random allocation and finite sampling variability must not be erased by calling this an ATE interval.

## 3. Computed exact examples, audited R49 events

All groups have six seats, 5:1 initial occupancy, a 5-round fixed future seat order `[0,1,2,3,4]`, initial A on seats 0–4 and B on seat 5, and the focal seat 0. All groups stayed during rounds 1–2; the private settlement is drawn *only* after round 2 and acknowledged. In each case, the entire prefix through round 4 is frozen. R53 must keep the focal’s first post-settlement switch as a known zero because seat 0 is never scheduled after round 2.

| Scenario | −4 observed rounds 1–4 | +4 observed rounds 1–4 | −4 possible cumulative excess | +4 possible cumulative excess | Δ bounds | Implication |
|---|---|---|---:|---:|---:|---|
| Robust ordering | stayed 5:1 | seat 2 changed to B at round 3; remained 4:2 | [12,15] | [6,9] | **[3,9]** | −4 strictly more congestion in all completions |
| Ambiguous ordering | stayed 5:1 | stayed 5:1 | [12,15] | [12,15] | **[−3,3]** | Both strict rankings can occur with the same frozen logs |

The separate finite-support enumerator confirms a subtlety that an interval alone hides: the **actual attainable gap values** in the robust example are exactly `{3,6,9}`, not every real number in `[3,9]`; in the reversible example they are exactly `{-3,0,3}`. Both sign reversals are attained. The additional exact-support DP accumulates discrete future costs over all legal six-mask choices and refuses to truncate large support sets without an explicit error. [Exact support code](p3/r49_rehearsal/r53_exact_support.py) · [tests](p3/r49_rehearsal/test_r53_exact_support.py).\n\nIn R49's half-point resource metric `2*excess + focal_switches`, the robust case's signed contrast is **[6,18]**; the focal never moves, so there is no extra resource-cost difference.

In the ambiguous case, if the actual final −4 decision is observed to STAY, the contrast shrinks to [0,3] (weak ranking); if the final +4 scheduled A seat is then observed moving to B, the contrast becomes [3,3] (strict ranking). Both additional observations reduce the set of feasible completions, so the lower bound is monotone nondecreasing and the upper bound monotone nonincreasing **along the actual recorded branch**. This monotonicity does not claim that observing a future choice will always make a strict ranking identifiable: ties can remain.

The program exports the per-arm aggregate sign classification, and returns explicit endpoint witnesses only under an **operator-only opt-in** because the original roster contains individual route histories and an unobserved future clock. All fractions use Python exact rational arithmetic; a float can obscure a true zero boundary.

Tests require exact endpoints, correct treatment denominators, a known no-focal-opportunity outcome, both-sign counterexamples, weak-boundary distinctions, monotone information refinement, and no treatment assignment for groups interrupted before round 2.

## 5. Prior-art adjudication

This is not a new general partial-identification theorem. The user's Drive **Manski (2005), *Partial Identification with Missing Data: Concepts and Findings*** sets out identification regions constructed from all possible missing-data distributions and explicitly separates identification from statistical inference. The user's Drive **Kline & Tamer (2023), *Recent Developments in Partial Identification*** distinguishes sharp identified sets from looser bounds and treats identified sets arising from economic choice models. [Manski 2005 Drive](https://drive.google.com/file/d/1dVBEhKzemt_qokxS-PTwntbq3RpLONdO/view) · [Kline & Tamer Drive](https://drive.google.com/file/d/1hiV8tPeR3qjCnkshzE7RJPZh5-RC-brD/view).

**High-priority not-found edition:** Sukjin Han (published online 2023; JASA 119(547), 2024, pp. 2000–2010), [*Optimal Dynamic Treatment Regimes and Partial Welfare Ordering*](https://doi.org/10.1080/01621459.2023.2238941), explicitly develops sharp partial welfare rankings of dynamic treatments using linear programs and an instrumental-variable framework. A freely available [author version](https://sukjinhan.github.io/Han_2023.pdf) and [journal landing page](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/01621459.2023.2238941) exist.
