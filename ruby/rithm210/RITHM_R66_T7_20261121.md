# RITHM-2.10 R66-T7 — Minimal Joint Revision Information, Sharp Welfare Identification and Exact Certificates

**Research date:** 2026-11-21. **Experimental status:** Exact synthetic/probability result; original-author data are not redistributed or used to infer new causal treatment effects. **GitHub branch:** `research/r66-t7-moment-identifiability-20261121`, separate from the user's one inexpensive Work session.

## Research question

In a finite n-actor revision game, suppose an analyst observes every player's switching probability and all pairwise joint moments, but not the entire 2^n-bit joint action distribution. How large an uncertainty interval remains for expected immediate congestion welfare and for future congestion following those switches? Which *minimum* order of joint moments makes a welfare functional identified?

### Exact Boolean-cube representation

For each binary revision vector x in {0,1}^n, the continuation welfare functional f(x) has a **unique** multi-affine representation

\[
 f(x)=\sum_{S\subseteq\{1,\ldots,n\}} c_S\prod_{i\in S}x_i,
 \qquad c_S=\sum_{T\subseteq S}(-1)^{|S|-|T|} f(1_T).
\]

Hence the expected welfare is the linear moment functional

\[
 E[f(X)]=\sum_S c_S\,P(X_i=1\ \forall i\in S).
\]

**Sufficiency theorem.** If the entire collection of joint moments through order d is known, E[f(X)] is point-identified for every admissible revision law if and only if c_S=0 for every |S|>d. This is linear algebra on the Boolean cube, *not* a novel probability theorem. A statistic that ignores exact mask composition does not automatically gain sufficiency just because its prediction error is small on 24 historical sessions.

### Congestion and long-horizon recovery

Use the original experiment's stage social cost C(n_A)=n_A²+2(6-n_A)²=24+3(n_A−4)². Consider a separate **stylized** correction mechanism: exactly K initial A→B switchers are brought back one at a time in subsequent periods. The recovery excess is

\[
 F(K)=3\sum_{j=1}^{K}j^2=\tfrac{K(K+1)(2K+1)}2.
\]

Immediate cost 3K² has Boolean degree 2; F(K) has degree 3 for n>=3. With three eligible A agents,

\[
 E[F(X_1+X_2+X_3)]=3\sum_i p_i+9\sum_{i<j}p_{ij}+6p_{123}.
\]

At p_i=1/2, p_ij=1/4, the triple joint probability is only known to satisfy 0<=p_123<=1/4, giving the **sharp welfare interval [45/4, 51/4] = [11.25,12.75]**. The even/odd parity distributions are attainable endpoints and have identical every individual and pairwise moment. Therefore **same means and covariance does not determine long-horizon welfare**. At H=1 the sharp interval collapses to exactly 9.

### Full binary moment optimization and dual certificates

For 2^n binary masks, define variables q_x>=0, sum_x q_x=1, and impose A q = b, where each row of A is the indicator that the actors in subset S all switch, |S|<=d. Optimize \(\min/\max\sum_x q_x f(x)\). This is a standard finite linear programming duality construction. SciPy/HiGHS proposes optima but our software **rationalizes primal and dual solutions and rejects them unless all constraints, inequalities and primal=dual objectives hold exactly**, so reported numbers have exact rational certificates. A numerical solver response alone is not accepted as a proof.

**Actual source-free 2026-11-21 computed stress test** with p_i=1/2 and p_ij=1/4 for all i≠j (all pairwise independent); full K-switch recovery (not always a physically admissible A4 crowd composition for n>4):

|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 3 | [9,9] | [45/4,51/4] = [11.25,12.75] | 12 | 1.5 |
| 4 | [15,15] | [43/2,47/2] = [21.5,23.5] | 22.5 | 2 |
| 5 | [22.5,22.5] | [35,40] | 37.5 | 5 |

**Scope:** n=3 and n=4 correspond to a possible number of A→B switchers at initial old n_A=4; n=5 and n=6 are *abstract dimensions* of the revision action cube and are not six eligible A→B departures from initial 4:2 in the real experiment.

A non-sharp interval follows by bounding unknown moments in [0,1], but the sharp interval explicitly respects **all joint-probability feasibility constraints**. That difference is a methodological contribution to RITHM's future empirical uncertainty reporting, not a claim to invent Boolean Fourier analysis or LP duality.

### Unidentified micro–macro mechanisms

These sharp intervals quantify what is unknowable from a selected set of moments **even when every observed moment is measured without error**. Actual human inference has additional sampling uncertainty, group dependence, self-selection and order confounding. For Full-late A4 the original study reported just 1 event with three or more A→B switchers among 257 opportunities (R66-T6), so trying to fit third-order moments from the original cohort would be strongly underpowered. This research does **not** claim human participants use a parity rule, that an information intervention causes synchronized switches, or that the synthetic correction protocol is fitted to humans.

The admissible information filtration for actual choices includes previously revealed own payoff at t and own prior choices, never future payoff or unavailable group occupancy. In the Endogenous condition, information purchase and experiment order are jointly confounded. The analyst may use group state only as a diagnostic or oracle, not as a fictional player observation.

### Dynamic transfer

For any two valid Markov transition matrices P and Q over a finite state space, with initial row μ and per-state welfare column ℓ,

\[
 J_H(Q)-J_H(P)=\sum_{k=0}^{H-2}\mu Q^k(Q-P)\Bigl(\sum_{j=0}^{H-2-k}P^j\ell\Bigr),
\]

where J_H(P)=sum_{t=0}^{H-1} μP^tℓ. Our Fraction-based implementation checks this for 1,2,3,5,12,40 periods. Together with Boolean Möbius expansion, this identifies **which state-sensitive continuation values amplify higher-order co-switching into social loss**.

### Social switching fee

Treat private perceived hassle (behavioral rule parameter) separately from an actual societal resource fee per realized switch f>=0. Total welfare cost J(f)=J(0)+f E[N_switch]. When candidate policies have the *same* expected total switches, f cancels exactly; the 3-switch parity counterexample retains its 1.5 welfare gap. Other policies have a precise break-even f. Neither f nor the synthetic correction protocol is measured from the original human sample.
