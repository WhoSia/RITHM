## Exact six-person macro loss identity

In the six-person two-route source game, let A occupancy be n and route congestion costs be c_A(n)=n and c_B(n)=2(6−n). Total congestion cost is C(n)=n²+2(6−n)²=24+3(n−4)²; the excess congestion cost is L(n)=3(n−4)².

Let X_i be the indicator that participant i changes their route, and s_i = −1 for prior A (A→B), +1 for prior B (B→A), with marginal p_i=E[X_i]. Since n'=n+Σs_i X_i, exact expansion yields:

\[
E[L(n')]=3\left[(n-4+\sum_i s_ip_i)^2+\sum_i p_i(1-p_i)+2\sum_{i<j}s_is_j\operatorname{Cov}(X_i,X_j)\right].
\]

This is the classical second-moment identity for signed Bernoulli sums. It implies that identical individual switch probabilities, mean net occupancy change and expected gross switching can coexist with different aggregate congestion cost. **Signed covariance**, not a simple yes/no claim about group cooperation, is what matters.

### Same-direction clock example

At previous n=5, five A actors each independently switch to B at probability 1/5, while one B actor never switches. The number changing K is Binomial(5,1/5), so E[L(5−K)]=12/5=2.4. Alternative: a shared all-or-none clock lets all five change with probability 1/5 and none change otherwise. The individual p_i and expected gross switching (one) are identical, but E[L(5−K)] = (4/5)×3+(1/5)×48 = **12**. The macro loss difference is **48/5=9.6**, a fivefold level increase.

For m same-direction eligible agents who update independently conditional on a common random rate R, relative to an independently updated law with the same fixed marginal E[R], a quadratic loss λ(n−n*)² produces the exact difference:

\[
\Delta E[L]=\lambda m(m-1)\operatorname{Var}(R).
\]

The source-free exact Fraction implementation verifies this identity by enumeration of 64 six-person switching masks, not by relying on a simulation approximation. This does not identify whether the actual source people use a shared revision clock.

### Opposite-direction counterexample

At previous n=4, one A and one B actor each switch with marginal 1/2. Independent flips produce E excess congestion **3/2**. Perfectly correlated flips, where both swap or neither swaps, preserve occupancy 4 and yield E excess **0**, with the same two actor marginals and the same expected gross switch count. Thus positive synchronization can HELP or HURT, depending on switch directions and the macro objective. If κ is a constant real cost per switch, it cancels in this comparison because expected gross switches match; changing κ's effect on decision probabilities is a separate behavioral question.

The five eligible actors in the first example are assumed to know a common previous occupancy snapshot to determine the attractive switch. **This would be an illegal actor-knowledge assumption for the original experiment's uninformed people**; it is a theoretical laboratory only.
