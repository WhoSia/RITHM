## A. Structural distinction is available in this experiment, unlike the 2023 fixed affine two-route payoff

For each route, group and round, matched the *entire contemporaneous vector of three route occupancies* across incident/nonincident conditions; used the median actual route cost to tolerate source irregularities. The resulting common-support observations were:

| Previously chosen route | Occupancy states present in **both** incident states | Median cost change under incident |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| 1 = A–B–D | 26 | 0 in all 26 |
| 2 = A–C–B–D | 22 | **+19 in all 22** |
| 3 = A–C–D | 26 | 0 in all 26 |

The manuscript describes the C–B link state as a cost change from **1 to 20**, consistent with this +19 route-specific shock. The realized payoff = 45 − incurred route cost. The separation is in the **objective realized cost**, not in any proven cognitive update channel.

**Formal filtration guard:** If a game's realized route cost is a deterministic function `c_i,t = g(r_i,t, N_t, Z_t)` of a publicly reported network state `(N_t, Z_t)` and own route `r_i,t`, then adjoining realized cost to the sigma-field generated by the complete reported network history gives no new observable information: `σ(r_≤t,N_≤t,Z_≤t,c_≤t)=σ(r_≤t,N_≤t,Z_≤t)`. Thus the exogenous +19 shock **separates objective cost from congestion at fixed N**, but **does not separate psychological reward learning from public-shock learning** when the shock and network conditions are also later observable. The three original cost anomalies below mean this is an ideal experimental-mechanism proposition, not an exact statement about every corrupted S2 cost cell.

## B. Behavioral contact: next-route switching after a very large actual cost shock

Primary: **NoInfo treatment**, whose users chose the full route prior to the incident state becoming known. Condition on *which route was chosen in the incident's round*; examine switching in the **next** round. This is a repeated, possibly endogenous treatment history, **not** an exclusion-valid randomized instrument for private reward-learning.

- Previous A–C–B–D (= shock-exposed route): **105** observations with preceding incident, **322** with no incident; next-route switching **63.81% versus 61.18%**; pooled +2.63 percentage points. Six equal-session-average contrasts **+2.76 percentage points**, exploratory six-session t5 95% CI **−12.18 to +17.71 pp**, only 2/6 session differences positive.
- Compare change for incident-exposed prior A–C–B–D choosers against the *mean contemporaneous incident-vs-nonincident contrasts* for prior A–B–D and A–C–D choosers, using each of 6 session contrasts: differential **+2.46 pp**, CI **−14.26 to +19.17 pp**, 2/6 positive. This is **not** a pure causal difference-in-differences: the network's full prior traffic conditions were shared and other paths' information may also update beliefs.
- Exploratory linear-probability regression adjusts for prior road, prior route occupancy, the user's previous switch, cubic round position and session intercepts; +incident interaction for route 2 is **+0.00660** on the probability scale with 6-session cluster-normal 95% interval **[−0.1297,+0.1429]**. With only six clusters, do not overtrust this asymptotic interval.
- **Ex-ante placebo:** In the NoInfo condition, current incident status was not available when choosing the current route. Difference in same-round *route 2 occupancy* between incidents and nonincidents averaged +0.50 participants (six-session exploratory interval −0.253 to +1.253); no precise contemporaneous imbalance detected. It is not proof of full randomized balance of all historical states.
- **Source-anomaly sensitivity:** remove all 8 NoInfo next-choice inputs whose previous same-route/round cost was ambiguously recorded in source; shock-exposed route 2 session contrast +2.69 pp instead of +2.76 pp. Direction/significance unchanged.
- Past accident-state versus same-round nonobservable accident-state placebo in next-switch samples had wide overlapping intervals: **no distinct causal behavioral signal is confirmed**.

**Verdict:** `EXOGENOUS_OBJECTIVE_COST_VS_OCCUPANCY_VARIATION = PASS`; `NEXT_ROUND_PRIVATE_REWARD_LEARNING_RESPONSE = NOT_ESTABLISHED`; `SHOCK_INSTRUMENT_EXCLUSION = HOLD`.

## C. Why the NoInfo between-group common residual persists: a sharper matched-pair falsifier

Take 12 NoInfo congestion groups, where **two groups sharing a laboratory session faced identical incident schedules but never congested one another**, and compare their time-demeaned cross-fitted route-count forecast residual vectors against **groups in other lab sessions with the same treatment order**.

First estimate each individual's next-route categorical probabilities in **six leave-one-entire-lab-session-out folds**, then aggregate `E_g,t = Σ_i [onehot(Y_i,t)−q_i,t]` across the 12 persons. For pairs `(g,h)` define the time-demeaned inner product over 19 post-initial periods `D(g,h)=mean_t[(E_g,t−mean E_g)·(E_h,t−mean E_h)]`.

| Personal probability model | Pair from SAME lab session | Pair from DIFFERENT lab session, matched order |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| Previously chosen route + coarse period | **+0.86125** | +0.03805 |
| Period + switching history + actual last personal cost | **+0.94603** | +0.01738 |
| Above plus analyst-observed previous shared accident flag | **+1.01862** | +0.01567 |
| Above plus two lagged shared accident flags | **+1.12152** | −0.01006 |

There are 6 actually paired session pairs and **24 distinct cross-session, same-order candidate group pairs**. Within each of two treatment-order strata are six groups (three physical sessions ×two groups); enumerate all **15 perfect matchings** per stratum, giving **225 order-stratified full reassignment patterns**. For the history+cost score, the observed same-lab average +0.94603 has upper-tail *matching-exchangeability diagnostic* `p=2/225=0.00889` (5/6 individually contrasted sessions positive). This is **not** an experimental randomization p-value: participants were not randomly assigned across laboratory-session clocks, matching within-session subjects share room/events/time, and model exploration reused the same data. The six session contrasts have one negative case that is retained.

**Interpretation:** The unexplained common residual is **session-specific** instead of merely a universal chronological learning trend shared across all laboratory sessions with the same order. Persisting after two accident lags and previous own cost does not prove peer coordination, nor prove that accident history is unimportant. Rival explanations include an unmeasured **common session environment/feedback**, collective expected values, continuous incident memory or conditional forecast miscalibration. The pairwise contrast does not observe any direct traffic game interaction across groups.

**Verdict:** `CROSS_SESSION_MATCHED_EXCESS_COMMON_SIGNAL = EXPLORATORY_PASS`; `ENDOGENOUS_PEER_COORDINATION = HOLD`; `COMMON_SHOCK_PLUS_FULL_NETWORK_FEEDBACK_RIVAL = OPEN`.

## D. Retained negative evidence and methodological priority

The very high +19 cost contrast, weak subsequent switch effect, and persistent same-session residual coupling jointly constrain a tempting but unjustified account that reward shocks cause systematic route switching that explains all coordination. That account is **NOT supported** by this evidence. This does not disprove slower-than-one-round learning or a different mechanism.
