## A. Foundations: utility, social choice, equilibrium, transactions
1. von Neumann & Morgenstern (1944), *Theory of Games and Economic Behavior*. [Edition record](https://openlibrary.org/books/OL59074745M/theory_of_games_and_economic_behavior). Utility and strategic rationality; historical foundations.
2. Gerard Debreu (1959), *Theory of Value*. [Cowles/Yale original monograph PDF](https://cowles.yale.edu/sites/default/files/2022-09/m17-all.pdf). Axiomatic price/equilibrium context.
3. Kenneth Arrow (1951), *Social Choice and Individual Values*. [Cowles/Yale original monograph PDF](https://cowles.yale.edu/sites/default/files/2022-09/m12-all.pdf). Individual-to-collective rationality mismatch.
4. Coase (1937), *The Nature of the Firm*. [DOI, publisher](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1468-0335.1937.tb00002.x). Transaction costs.
5. George Stigler (1961), *The Economics of Information*. [DOI, publisher](https://doi.org/10.1086/258464). Costly search.
6. Oliver Williamson (1985), *The Economic Institutions of Capitalism*. [Library catalog](https://search.worldcat.org/title/The-economic-institutions-of-capitalism-%3A-firms-markets-relational-contracting/oclc/12216444). Institutional governance and transaction friction.
7. Savage (1954), *The Foundations of Statistics*. Bibliographic verification pending; do not infer a free PDF license.

## B. Bounded rationality and behavioral rivals
8. Herbert Simon (1955), *A Behavioral Model of Rational Choice*. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.2307/1884852). Satisficing and limited cognition.
9. Ariel Rubinstein (1998), *Modeling Bounded Rationality*. [MIT Press](https://mitpress.mit.edu/9780262681001/modeling-bounded-rationality/). Choice procedures, memory, computability.
10. Tversky & Kahneman (1974), *Judgment under Uncertainty: Heuristics and Biases*. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.185.4157.1124). Judgment heuristics are not automatically a model of transaction friction.
11. Rabin (1998), *Psychology and Economics*. [Harvard author page](https://rabin.scholars.harvard.edu/publications/psychology-and-economics). Behavioral contrasts to textbook rationality.
12. Gigerenzer, Todd et al. (1999), *Simple Heuristics That Make Us Smart*. [Book metadata](https://books.google.com/books/about/Simple_Heuristics_that_Make_Us_Smart.html?id=9RNF8TUmRywC). Ecological/fast-frugal rationality.
13. David Laibson (1997), *Golden Eggs and Hyperbolic Discounting*. [Harvard open manuscript](https://dash.harvard.edu/entities/publication/73120378-8460-6bd4-e053-0100007fdf3b). Dynamic inconsistency and commitment technology.
14. Stahl & Wilson (1995), *On Players' Models of Other Players*. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1006/game.1995.1031). Limited opponent-model computation.

## C. Costly thinking and resource-rational reasoning
15. Russell & Wefald (1991), *Principles of Metareasoning*. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/0004-3702(91)90015-C). Value of computation.
16. Barton Lipman (1991), *How to Decide How to Decide How to...: Modeling Limited Rationality*. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.2307/2938176). Meta-choice regress: who chooses the thinking algorithm?
17. Christopher Sims (2003), *Implications of Rational Inattention*. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0304-3932(03)00029-1). Information-processing capacity and attention cost.
18. Griffiths, Lieder & Goodman (2015), *Rational Use of Cognitive Resources*. [Open publisher page](https://doi.org/10.1111/tops.12142). Levels of cognitive models.
19. Lieder & Griffiths (2019), *Resource-Rational Analysis: Understanding Human Cognition as the Optimal Use of Limited Computational Resources*. [Publisher](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0140525X1900061X). Resource rationality.
20. Rahnev (2020), *Resource-Rational Analysis versus Resource-Rational Humans*. [Open full text](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7702215/). Research-method/model versus empirical assertion critique.
21. Papadimitriou (2001), *Algorithms, Games, and the Internet*. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1145/380752.380883). Algorithmic game theory context.
22. Wijayaratna et al. (2017), human route-choice data original article. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0184191). RITHM prior existing human-source context.

## RITHM research questions & reading order
1. **Foundation (1,2,4,5,8):** Which rationality assumptions omit costs of computation and policy execution?
2. **Procedure (9,15,16,17,18):** Model a *bounded algorithm* with costly observation of hidden states; avoid circular pre-known value of calculation.
3. **Human rivals (10,11,12,13,19,20):** Detect confounding of latent preferences, memory, inattention and actual switching constraints.
4. **Collective feedback (3,14,21,22):** Compare resource-rational individual strategy updates with aggregate congestion and temporal stability.
5. **Proof/identification gate:** Exactly establish finite-state assumptions and measurability, then test cost–stability phase transitions with independent Ruby/Go and seek novelty in specialized literature. No human causal claim without suitable data.
